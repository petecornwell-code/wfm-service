<objective>
Make the seat-supply gate see what it is actually measuring.

G-15-25, measured not inferred: five break bands were added at the envelope edges of the three live
weekend templates, the solve was attempted, the ORIGINAL bands were restored, and the solve was
attempted again. Both runs produced byte-identical gate output — same five dates, same figures,
2026-01-11 "reaches 136" in both. The cause is structural: the supply computation asks
`pairs.stream().anyMatch(p -> p.covers(ts))` over the DESK-WIDE pair list. Each band pair excludes
only its own break hour, so a template with three or more bands already unions to its FULL envelope,
and adding further bands can never change a saturated union. The gate cannot see band composition at
all.

That matters because the union models "some agent could work this hour" while the solver binds each
agent to ONE pair. Two libraries with identical envelopes and completely different break structure —
one solvable, one not — are indistinguishable to this gate. It is measuring the wrong quantity, not
merely measuring it on the wrong calendar. Plan 15-18's date filter does not touch this; the gap says
so in its own `fix`.

This plan also carries G-15-31's disposition, because plan 15-19 produced the analysis that gap
demanded and someone has to act on it — or decline to, in writing.

Purpose: a supply figure computed against each agent-day's own eligible pairs, proven sensitive to
band composition by the exact experiment that proved the old one blind, with false refusals measured
against a corpus rather than hoped away.

Output: the per-agent-day supply model in the gate, a band-composition red-proof, a false-refusal
measurement, the analysis's recommendation acted on or declined in writing, and the last two gap
entries closed.
</objective>
