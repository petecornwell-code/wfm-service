<objective>
Make the suggested library a draft an operator can accept unchanged without walking into two known
traps.

Test 9 recorded the generator's remaining defects with live evidence, and both are still open:

1. DUPLICATE TEMPLATES. `greedyCover` legitimately selects two same-span candidates with different
   band offsets so each covers the other's break hour (D-02 self-cover). Expanding both to the same
   three bands collapses them into an identical duplicate. Observed live: the weekday cluster
   contained 08:00-17:00 twice with identical bands — one wasted stagger position on a desk whose
   entire problem is absorbing 315 surplus agent-hours.

2. BAND PLACEMENT IGNORES DEMAND. Offsets are chosen for coverage, never to avoid the demand peak.
   Observed live: it proposed breaking at 11:00 on the 10:00-19:00 weekend template — the busiest
   weekend hour at 44 FTE Saturday and 32 Sunday. The operator discarded the generated bands and
   hand-set 13:00-15:00 instead, which is the feature failing at its stated job: answering "how will
   a user know what shifts to set?"

Purpose: a generated draft whose break positions are defensible against the desk's own demand curve,
with no wasted duplicate, and with the cover guarantee still measured rather than assumed.

Output: dedupe after band selection, demand-aware band placement bounded away from the envelope
edges, and a round-trip guard tying the generator's output to the validator that judges it.
</objective>

  <behavior>
    - Two selected candidates sharing a span and weekdays but differing only in their coverage-bearing
      offset, once expanded to identical band sets, produce ONE emitted template, not two.
    - Two selected candidates sharing a span whose expanded band sets DIFFER produce two templates —
      dedupe is on exact identity, never on span alone.
    - After dedupe, emitted template names are contiguous (`Suggested 1`, `Suggested 2`, ... with no
      gap), so a collapsed duplicate does not leave a hole in the numbering an operator would read as
      a missing row.
    - The `uncoveredDetails` in the response is computed against the bands actually emitted, and on a
      coverable desk is empty.
    - The suggestion remains deterministic across two calls on an unchanged desk.
  </behavior>

  <action>
Move the uncovered-window computation so it describes the emitted draft. Today `uncoveredDetails` is
derived from the pre-expansion single-band candidates; recompute it from the final emitted templates
and their final band lists, using the same `ShiftLibraryValidationService.covers` call that
`stillUncovered` already uses — one predicate, still exactly one implementation (D-08). This must
happen after dedupe, so the reported list and the returned templates can never disagree, which is
the property D-08 exists to protect and which Task 2 makes load-bearing rather than merely tidy.

Then dedupe. Do it in `buildResponse` AFTER `suggestedBands` has run for every selected candidate,
because that is where two distinct candidates collapse into one identical template. Key on the tuple
that makes two emitted templates operationally indistinguishable: start time, end time, the sorted
valid-weekday list, and the full ordered band list of (offset, duration, capacity). Keep the first
occurrence in the existing deterministic iteration order and drop later exact matches. Assign the
`Suggested N` index AFTER dedupe so the numbering is contiguous.

Record in a comment why dedupe is safe for coverage and why it must be exact-match: two templates
identical on that tuple cover exactly the same windows, so removing one cannot change any
`covers()` result; two templates sharing only a span do NOT, and collapsing them would silently
discard a stagger position the supply expansion deliberately added.

Note also, in the same comment, what dedupe does NOT do: it does not backfill the lost template.
`expandForSupply` counts distinct SPANS (via `spanKey`), so a collapsed duplicate never reduced the
span count and the supply-aware target is unaffected — but the draft does come back with one fewer
row than the pre-dedupe expansion produced, and that is the correct answer rather than a shortfall
to be papered over.
  </action>

  <behavior>
    - On a desk whose demand has a sharp peak strictly inside a template's envelope, no emitted band's
      break window overlaps the peak hour, provided at least `SUGGESTED_BAND_COUNT` admissible
      offsets avoiding it exist.
    - When the envelope is too short to avoid the peak with the required number of bands, the
      generator still emits the full band count (capacity arithmetic depends on it) and prefers the
      lowest-demand admissible offsets available — it degrades, it does not throw and does not drop
      bands.
    - Every emitted offset lies within the existing admissible range, so no band's break window
      touches the envelope's first or last slot.
    - The emitted band set covers every window the pre-expansion coverage-bearing candidate covered.
    - Two calls on an unchanged desk produce byte-identical drafts, including band offsets — ties in
      demand are broken deterministically.
    - A break-less candidate (duration zero, below `breakMinShiftHours`) still emits zero bands.
  </behavior>

  <action>
Thread the demand curve into band selection. `suggestedBands` currently receives `hoursByWeekday`,
which is CONTRACTED hours and says nothing about demand; the curve it needs is the per-cluster
`demand` list already in scope in `generateSuggestion`. Pass a per-weekday, per-start-time FTE view
of that cluster's demand down through `buildResponse` into `suggestedBands`. Reuse the same
weekday/start-time aggregation shape `clusterWeekdaysByDemandShape` already builds rather than
inventing a second one.

Replace the outward walk with a demand-ranked selection over the admissible offsets:

- Enumerate every grid-aligned offset in the existing admissible range. Keep those bounds exactly as
  they are — they are what forbids an edge break, and Test 10's caveat plus the operator's ruling
  make relaxing them a regression, not an improvement. Note this in a comment so a future reader
  does not "fix" the bounds to gain search room.
- Score each offset by the demand its break window would sit on, summed over the break window's
  timeslots and taken as the MAXIMUM across the template's valid weekdays — the same
  busiest-day-not-average-day rule `suggestedCapacity` already applies to headcount, and for the same
  reason: a break that empties the peak on one day is not excused by being harmless on another.
- Choose exactly `SUGGESTED_BAND_COUNT` offsets in ascending score order, breaking ties by ascending
  offset so the result is deterministic (D-11's identical-draft-on-an-unchanged-desk property).
- Then check coverage: if the chosen set does not cover every window the pre-expansion
  coverage-bearing band covered, put the coverage-bearing offset back, evicting the
  highest-scoring chosen offset, and re-check. Coverage is the harder constraint and wins; the
  demand bias is a preference applied within it.
- If fewer admissible offsets exist than `SUGGESTED_BAND_COUNT`, emit what fits, exactly as the
  current implementation already does for a short span.

Do not change `SUGGESTED_BAND_COUNT` and do not change `suggestedCapacity`. The capacity arithmetic
is sized against the band COUNT and both of its bounding constraints — total capacity must exceed
headcount because `bandCapacityWeight` is `ofHard(1)` and under-sizing makes the desk unsolvable,
while no single band may admit more than half the shift or the concentration advisory fires. Moving
offsets does not disturb that; changing the count would.
  </action>
