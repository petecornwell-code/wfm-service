<assumption_delta_decision>
The deterministic assumption-delta scan on this phase returns `detected: true`, family `chosen`: a
value that was a derived constant becomes a chosen parameter. The day boundary was the constant
`00:00`, hard-coded into `DayWindow`'s `00:00`-by-position rule; after this phase it is a per-desk
declared value, and a timeslot that carried one date now carries two.

- **Primary noun:** `business_date`. The business day is the identity a timeslot's scheduling
  behaviour hangs on; the calendar date is demoted to a storage and rendering detail of the
  `00:00` variant.
- **Decision:** `promote` — not `add-alongside`. The promotion is executed by SOLV-01 and enforced by
  SOLV-02's join guard in Phase 20; this phase establishes the primary noun and the single deriving
  writer (BDAY-08) that make the promotion possible. Phase 18's coexistence of the two columns is a
  transitional state with a named, requirement-backed closure, not accepted debt.
- **What would force a later promote if this were wrong:** a constraint joining on calendar date
  where business date is meant. That is precisely the silent non-join REQUIREMENTS.md names as this
  milestone's characteristic failure mode, and SOLV-02 is the guard that makes it loud.
- **Companion invariant test:** BDAY-06's D-17 structural predicates are written to be callable
  against any `Schedule`'s facts (plan 18-04), so a future phase reintroducing the singular
  calendar-date assumption trips them. No additional task is needed here.
