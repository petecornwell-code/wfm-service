<objective>
Make a waiver actually waive something, through exactly one predicate that three consumers share.

`agent_rest_waiver` rows become their own solver problem fact. Both mode-gated constraints exclude a
pair whose successor business date carries a waiver. The comparison lives in one place, and a
structural guard fails the build if a second implementation of it ever appears.

Purpose: D-03 bought two separate constraints over one fused stream, with the divergence cost named.
D-08 is what pays for that choice: with three things that must agree about what "waived" means — the
SHIFT constraint, the SLOT constraint and REST-03's pre-solve refusal — a shared predicate plus a
guard is the only mechanism stopping them drifting apart. That drift is invisible until it has already
happened, which is why the guard is load-bearing rather than belt-and-braces.

D-06 fixes the direction: a waiver on business date D waives the rest **coming into** D. The deciding
argument was the horizon edge — a violation on the first day of a period pairs a pre-horizon day 0
against day 1, and only this reading lets an operator waive a date that exists in the schedule at all.
Waiving either end touching D was rejected because one waiver would then clear two distinct pairs, and
REST-07's report could no longer say which waiver cleared which violation.

Output: `RestWaiverLookup`, `Schedule.agentRestWaivers`, the batched read in `SolverService`, the
exclusion clause in both constraints, the guard registry file, and the guard test.
</objective>
