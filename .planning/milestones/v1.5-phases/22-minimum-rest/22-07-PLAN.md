<objective>
Refuse the solve up front where the roster makes a rest violation unavoidable, and say exactly who and
which two shifts.

A new `requireRestFeasibility` in `SolverService`, built on `requireShiftEnvelopeSeatSupply`'s proven
shape: per-date analysis, an accumulated `ErrorDetail` list naming entities, one
`PreSolveValidationException` carrying all of them, and a separate non-blocking warnings channel
alongside.

Purpose: REST-03 asks for a mechanism genuinely separate from the in-solve constraint, not a
hoped-for side effect of it. Without it, the case most likely to be structurally impossible — a night
shift rolling into a new period — surfaces as residual hard score on a project whose desks do not
reliably solve to hard 0 anyway, so it is indistinguishable from ordinary difficulty. A genuinely
impossible agent-day also burns the entire solve, and solver state here is heap-only.

The predicate is harder than the seat-supply precedent and that difference is the whole plan. The
seat-supply gate asks a per-date existence question. This asks a cross-date product question: does
**every** combination of (eligible pair on D-1, eligible pair on D) violate the minimum?
`requireShiftEnvelopeSeatSupply` already holds both days' rows and both days' value ranges, so the
data is in scope; the predicate is not.

Output: `requireRestFeasibility` covering both scheduling modes, the updated predicate-guard registry,
and the test class that distinguishes "hard violation, solvable with a different choice" from
"structurally impossible, refused".
</objective>
