<objective>
Settle, with the operator, what to do about nine build-allowlisted raw time comparisons whose
stated justification stops being true the moment a desk has a non-midnight day start.

Purpose: the structural time-arithmetic guard permits nine raw `isBefore` / `isAfter` /
`compareTo` calls on scheduling times, each with a written reason. Every one of those reasons is a
variant of the same argument: *both operands are START times, so the end-of-day ambiguity does not
apply.* That argument is correct about the end-boundary trap and silent about ordering. Two start
times compared raw order by the clock, not by position in the business day — so on a desk anchored
at 21:00, a `22:00` start is "before" a `06:00` start, when it is in fact eight hours earlier in
the same business day, and the comparison returns the opposite of the truth.

This was found by reading the allowlist during planning. It is in no decision, no requirement and
no research pitfall — but it is the same defect class as the solver's three collapsed scan ranges,
the generator's inverted candidate sweep, the export's clock-ordered rows and the grid's seven
sites, and this phase is what makes a non-midnight desk operator-reachable for the first time. Two
of the nine touch solver scoring and move ordering, which is why the planner is not deciding it
alone.

Output: a recorded decision, and either the conversions or the amended justifications that follow
from it.
</objective>
