<objective>
Settle, with the operator, which date system the two Erlang staffing calculators' clear-before-insert
range means — and then make the record say so.

Purpose: `StaffingRequirementRepository` carries two range-delete twins that differ in exactly one
place, one filtering a timeslot's calendar date and one its business date. The demand-upload path
already uses the business-date twin. The two Erlang calculators still use the calendar-date one,
and the inline comment at that method says why: their `from`/`to` arrive from the request payload
as operator-facing calendar dates, and re-pointing them would silently re-scope two operator-facing
endpoints. Phase 20 left them there on the explicit grounds that no desk could carry a non-midnight
anchor yet, making them "unreachable instances of the same latent defect today, not live ones" —
and handed them to this phase.

Plan 21-02 is what ends that unreachability. So this is the phase where the premise expires, and
both the phase CONTEXT and the phase RESEARCH say the same thing about it: raise it at planning as
an explicit scope decision, because it was surfaced and never settled.

Output: a recorded decision, and the code or the documentation that follows from it.
</objective>

<task type="checkpoint:decision" gate="blocking-human">
  <decision>Do the two Erlang staffing calculators' `from`/`to` range bounds mean calendar dates or business dates?</decision>
  <context>
Both calculators clear existing live staffing requirements across `[from, to]` and then insert one
row per request item, keyed by an explicit `timeslotId`. The clear is by date range; the inserts
are by id. On a desk whose day starts at 21:00 those two can disagree: a timeslot belonging to
business day D carries calendar date D+1 for every one of its post-midnight slots, so a clear
scoped to calendar `[D, D]` misses rows the operator is about to replace for business day D, and
reaches rows belonging to business day D-1 that the operator never mentioned.

Until plan 21-02 lands, no desk an operator can reach has a non-midnight anchor, so the two
readings produce identical rows on every desk that exists. 21-02 makes a 21:00 desk creatable from
the desk page, which is what turns this from latent into live — in the same phase, potentially in
the same session.

What makes this a decision rather than a default: `from` and `to` arrive from the request payload
as dates an operator typed into the staffing screen. Changing which date system they mean changes
what that operator's own input does to stored demand. That is a user-visible semantic change to
two endpoints, and it is not a change the planner should make unilaterally — the phase CONTEXT and
the phase RESEARCH both say so in those words.

The undo cost either way is small (one method name per call site, plus a test), so this gate buys
the decision, not the reversibility.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="migrate-now">
      <name>Migrate both calculators to the business-date twin in this plan</name>
      <pros>Closes the gap in the same phase that creates it, so no window exists in which a
      reachable 21:00 desk can mis-scope an Erlang recalculation. Makes all three write paths
      (demand save, Erlang C, Erlang X) agree on one date system, which is the property this
      milestone keeps finding the absence of. The fix is two method names and one test — the
      smallest the change will ever be. Both the phase CONTEXT and the phase RESEARCH recommend
      it.</pros>
      <cons>The two endpoints' `from`/`to` quietly start meaning business dates. On a 21:00 desk
      an operator entering a single date now clears a window that begins at 21:00 the previous
      calendar day, which is correct but is not what the screen's date picker visually
      suggests. No copy change is in this phase's scope to explain it.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="defer-with-record">
      <name>Keep calendar-date semantics and record the deferral with a named owner</name>
      <pros>Neither operator-facing endpoint changes behaviour in a phase whose stated job is
      overnight templates. The staffing screen's date picker keeps meaning exactly what it
      appears to mean. Any future migration can arrive together with the copy change that
      explains it.</pros>
      <cons>Ships a known defect that this phase itself makes reachable: on a 21:00 desk, an
      Erlang recalculation can leave stale demand rows for the business day it was asked to
      replace, and silently clear rows for the business day before it. Nothing fails to report
      this — it is a wrong row set, not an error. It must then be written down as a new,
      knowingly-accepted gap in this phase's deferred items rather than left as an inherited
      open note.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Reply `migrate-now` or `defer-with-record`. If you want something other than these two — for example migrating only one of the two calculators, or migrating plus a copy change on the staffing screen — say so and the plan will be revised rather than forced into one of these.</resume-signal>
