<objective>
Close gap N-2: the Agent Allocation "Required" and "Over / under" rows compare demand looked up by
CALENDAR date with a grid keyed by BUSINESS date, and the demand fetch filters on calendar dates, so
on a desk whose day start is not `00:00` post-midnight cells show the next business day's demand or
nothing at all on the final day. Fixed additive-then-consume (Phase 19 D-10 / Phase 20 precedent): the
response gains `businessDate` (the `date` field is not touched), the list endpoint gains a server-side
business-date range, and the one consumer switches to both. Then the phase gate runs.

Purpose: the backend is proven first by a service test (D-09) because the frontend has no test runner
(Phase 22 WR-04, deferred); the UI fix is then measured live on a throwaway local stack.

Output: one additive record component, two repository queries, two request params and their refusal
rules, a new backend test class, a two-line frontend consumer switch, a recorded live measurement, and
the phase's single full-suite gate.

No UI-SPEC applies (operator decision 2): no layout or interaction changes, only which key the existing
rows look demand up by.
</objective>

<assumption_delta_decision>
primary_noun: A staffing-requirement row now carries two dates. The BUSINESS date (`businessDate`) is the key for every join and lookup — the schedule grid, the allocation rows, the business-range filter. The CALENDAR date (`date`) is the operator-facing "when does this happen" label.
decision: add-alongside
rationale: `date` is a published response contract whose calendar meaning Phase 20 D-10 / SOLV-07 deliberately fixed; CONTEXT D-03 rates the new field `costly` (removing it later breaks any consumer that adopted it). Additive-then-consume keeps every existing caller byte-compatible while the one consumer that needs the business key gets it.
accepted_debt: two date fields on one row, and two pairs of range params (`from`/`to` calendar, `businessFrom`/`businessTo` business) on one endpoint. What would force a later promote: a consumer that needs only one date (e.g. an external integration that cannot be taught which to use), or a decision that operator-facing lists should also be business-keyed — either would reopen D-10 as its own decision, not as an extension of this one.
companion_invariant: StaffingRequirementListBusinessRangeTest asserts that on a 06:00 desk every item's businessDate equals DayWindow.businessDateOf(06:00, date, startTime), and on a 00:00 desk equals date — the two fields can never silently disagree with the single derivation.
</assumption_delta_decision>
