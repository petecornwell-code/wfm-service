<objective>
Give the first day of a solving period a real predecessor, and make the last day's silence a decision
rather than an accident.

The solve looks back exactly one business date, at ACCEPTED dates only, through one batched
date-filtered query per mode. Those spans become their own problem fact, and both mode-gated
constraints concat them onto their in-horizon predecessor stream, so one filter and one measurement
serve both pair sources.

Purpose: REST-05 asks for defined, tested behaviour at both horizon edges rather than an accidental
one. Without the lookback, the case most likely to be a genuine rest violation — a night shift rolling
from an accepted period into a new one — is invisible, and the first day of every period is silently
unconstrained. D-11 then makes the end edge safe rather than merely empty: the last day's outgoing
rest is enforced later, when the next period's solve treats this one as its day-0 predecessor under
D-10. The lookback is what makes that true, which is why no forward lookahead is built.

Both repository reads this needs are new. Neither `AgentAssignmentRepository` nor
`AgentShiftAssignmentRepository` has a date-filtered read on HEAD — both only fetch a whole schedule.
On a SLOT desk `agent_assignment` holds one row per assigned seat per slot, so a month on a large desk
is six figures of rows. A date-filtered query is a precondition here, not an optimisation.

Output: two repository reads, `RestPredecessorService`, `Schedule.priorRestSpans`, the concat in both
constraints, and the two test classes that pin both edges.
</objective>
