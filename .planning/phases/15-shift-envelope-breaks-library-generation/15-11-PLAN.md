<objective>
Close the D1 half of G-15-10 by making in-envelope seat supply a CHECKED PRECONDITION of every
shift-mode solve rather than something the model hopes for.

**Position taken on the deepest design question — the D1 zero-slack equality stays.** The value
range in `AgentShiftAssignment.getEligibleShiftBandPairs` admits only pairs whose net hours exactly
equal the agent-day's effective hours (D-04), and `ShiftBandPair.covers` excludes the break window,
so legal slots equal expected work slots exactly and an agent must occupy 100% of their legal slots.
Three reasons not to relax it with a tolerance:

1. **It is the definition of a shift.** An agent assigned an 8-hour-net shift works those 8 hours in
   that window. A tolerance admits a pair whose legal slots outnumber the agent's contracted slots,
   so some legal slots go unworked and nothing decides which. The break-geometry lane measured that
   directly: shift mode scores a scattered geometry and an edge geometry IDENTICALLY across all
   eight reachable constraints, while slot mode ranks them strictly. There is no gradient defending
   geometry once the equality is loosened, so a tolerance trades ENVL-04's contiguity guarantee — a
   phase success criterion that currently holds only because held equals legal — for slack.
2. **It would not close the arithmetic.** Slack lets an agent skip a legal slot; it does not create a
   seat. If in-library supply is short by N agent-slots, those N still land on a hard constraint —
   `Contracted hours (under)` at `ofHard(100)`. That is exactly the relocation already measured and
   refuted for the weight-raising remedy, which moved a -4 envelope score to -400 on contracted
   hours. Relaxing the equality is the same mistake wearing a different hat.
3. **It weakens a deliberate guard.** The same exact-equality filter is what makes an hours mismatch
   degrade to an unassigned shift (D-06) rather than seating an agent on a shift they cannot work.

So the equality is correct and the real error is that a solve is permitted to START when supply
cannot meet it. **That is what this plan fixes.**

**Why the gate is still needed after operator ruling OR-1.** Suppressing filler seats at hours the
library does not reach (plan 15-09) removes the specific out-of-envelope DESTINATION, but it restores
no slack. If in-library supply is genuinely short, the deficit does not vanish — it migrates from
`Shift envelope compliance` at `ofHard(1)` to `Contracted hours (under)` at `ofHard(100)`. Without
this plan, 15-09 alone would convert a -19 into something like -1900: a larger, equally unactionable
number. The gate is what converts it into a sentence an operator can act on. It is load-bearing, not
defence in depth.

**Why refusal rather than manufacturing more seats.** Seats already exist up to the operator's own
over-allocation limit — `expandOverflowAssignments` tops every demanded timeslot to that ceiling.
Manufacturing seats beyond it would not create feasibility; filling them would be charged by
`Bulk over-allocation limit`, so the deficit would simply relocate a third time. The over-allocation
limit is the operator's policy, and quietly widening it inside the solver would be deciding their
staffing policy for them. Naming it as one of the levers keeps them in control.

Purpose: turn an irreducible, mislabelled hard score into an operator-actionable refusal, and pin the
zero-slack identity as a deliberate invariant with a test that says so.
Output: a shift-mode seat-supply gate, a promoted invariant test, and a gate test proving both the
refusal and the absence of false refusals.

**Not in this plan:** raising `shiftEnvelopeComplianceWeight` (see Task 3 — the tie is recorded, not
changed), any change to the value-range filter, and save-time envelope-containment validation in
`ShiftTemplateService` (deferred alongside D5 — see plan 15-13).
</objective>
