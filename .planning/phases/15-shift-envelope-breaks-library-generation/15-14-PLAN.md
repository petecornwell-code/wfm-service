<objective>
Give solver tuning an automated guard that cannot itself be flaky.

Two sessions have paid for its absence. An acceptor change (`0hard -> 1hard`) regressed the live
desk sevenfold, -9 to -66, and shipped CI-green because no fixture in 590 tests is sensitive to
acceptor behaviour (G-15-22). A weight-tuning session burned ~12 live solves producing almost no
usable signal, because hard scores were being compared across weight changes where they are not
comparable (G-15-29).

The naive fix is worse than nothing. A benchmark asserting a hard-score ceiling WILL be flaky, and
this is measured rather than feared: on 2026-09-01, byte-identical configuration verified field by
field through the API, twenty minutes apart on the same build and the same library, gave
`b88cc98f 0 hard FEASIBLE` and `60523b98 -20 hard NOT FEASIBLE`. Earlier the same file records
envelope violations of 2, 3, 3, 4, 6, 8 across runs that all met every operator requirement, with
no consistent ordering by configuration. A test asserting `hardScore >= -N` on one run fails
randomly and gets deleted by the third person it annoys — the exact fate `BreakAwareConstructionTest`
is already drifting toward (`deferred-items.md`: "the assertion is wall-clock bounded, so it
measures hardware as well as solver quality").

What WAS stable across all three of 2026-09-01's live runs — including the -120 run at a
deliberately wrong over-allocation limit — is a set of structural invariants: 0 split shifts across
138 agent-days, 0 edge breaks, every edge hour non-zero on every day. They are enforced by
constraints rather than found by search, and they discriminate: the -120 run held all three while
failing on score, and the pre-fix build violated splits on 24 of 138 agent-days (17%).

Purpose: make the guard invariant-based, so it is decidable; make every solve seeded and
step-count terminated, so it is deterministic; and put it in the DEFAULT suite, so it actually runs.

Output: a live-shape synthetic fixture, a five-seed harness over the shipped `solverConfig.xml`,
three structural walkers, and a failure report that tells the next person to compare violation
counts rather than raw hard scores.
</objective>

<design_decisions>

Made here, concretely, rather than deferred to the executor. Each carries its evidence.

**P-37 — Fixture: synthetic, but shaped verbatim from the live Stubhub (EN) library.**
Not a captured dump of the live desk. Two reasons, both already on the record. (1) The debug lane
established that "a 400-agent/60-day benchmark would have missed G-15-10 identically, because the
fixture missed it by SHAPE, not scale" (`ShiftDeskEndToEndRegressionTest` class javadoc). Shape is
what discriminates. (2) `15-BENCHMARK.md`'s T-15-32 provenance note settles the data question for
this project: benchmark fixtures read no real tenant row, use `Agent-N` / `A-N` identifiers, and
that is "a stronger mitigation than filtering PII from real data after the fact." The SHAPE,
however, is transcribed from `HANDOFF.md` §2/§3 exactly, because that is the shape the three
invariants were measured stable against.

**P-38 — The library carries a slack template, deliberately.**
`Weekend Flex 10:00-20:00` has a 10h envelope with a 1h band: net 9h against an 8h contract. That
one hour of slack is what G-15-27 measured 23 of 30 splits on. Without it a split shift is not
representable at all (zero-slack templates force the agent to occupy 100% of their legal slots) and
the contiguity invariant would be vacuously true. The guard's most important invariant would prove
nothing. This is load-bearing, not incidental.

**P-39 — Band offsets stay at least two slots clear of both envelope edges.**
Every band in the live library already satisfies this (minimum observed margin: 120 minutes).
It is stated as a fixture rule because the edge-break invariant asks whether the agent works on
BOTH sides of their break, and with one hour of slack in play a band placed one slot from an
envelope edge could legitimately end up with no worked slot on one side. That would make the
invariant flaky for a legal schedule — the precise failure mode this whole plan exists to avoid.

**P-40 — Demand is derived from a provably-satisfiable ideal assignment, not hand-tuned.**
Two holders per template, each holder taking a different band, each working their template's legal
non-break hours truncated to the 8-hour contract from the end. Per-hour demand is the count of
ideal holders working that hour. Total daily demand is therefore exactly `agentCount x contracted
slots`, a feasible assignment provably exists, and the edge hours get demand only their sole-route
template can serve. `15-BENCHMARK.md`'s comparative fixture is tight in exactly this way ("exactly
`agentCount` seats per timeslot — no idle seat headroom at all") and the shift arm still reached
`0hard` on 5 of 5 seeds, so tightness is not a convergence risk in shift mode.

**P-41 — Weights are pinned to the LIVE values, and the shipped defaults are pinned by a separate
assertion.** The invariants were measured stable under `HANDOFF.md` §2's live settings
(`shiftEnvelopeCompliance` 10, `shiftWorkContiguity` 10, `unassignedAssignment` 10000, `bandCapacity`
1) with over/under 500/50, so the guard reproduces that configuration. The shipped no-arg defaults
diverge from it (`shiftEnvelopeComplianceWeight` is `ofHard(1)` by deliberate Phase 15 decision;
`unassignedAssignmentWeight` is `ofSoft(1000)`, and HANDOFF §4 records that 1000 collapsed weekend
coverage on the live desk). Losing default-regression sensitivity is unacceptable, so it is bought
back cheaply by a separate assertion pinning the four defaults to their current values.

**P-42 — Score-shaped assertions are on violation COUNTS, median across five seeds.**
G-15-29's `fix:` permits a score-based assertion only "on violation counts across repeated runs."
The rule is committed here, before any number exists (the P-36 discipline `15-BENCHMARK.md`
established): the ceiling is the median across the five seeds observed at the baseline commit, plus
a headroom of 2, floored at 2 when the baseline median is 0. It is a coarse trip-wire sized to catch
the sevenfold acceptor class of regression, not a fine-grained quality metric — the invariants are
the real gate. Median-of-five means one noisy seed can never fail it.

**P-43 — The guard runs in the default `./gradlew test`, ungated.**
`ShiftModelBenchmarkTest` is gated on `-Dwfm.benchmark=true` and therefore never runs in the deploy
gate — which is exactly why a green build shipped the acceptor regression. A new gate behind a new
flag would repeat that. `deploy.yml`'s Test job runs `./gradlew build`, so an ungated test is on the
deploy gate automatically, with no workflow change. Runtime is budgeted and measured (Task 3).

**P-44 — Elapsed time is printed, never asserted.**
`BreakAwareConstructionTest` is the live counter-example in this repo: a wall-clock-bounded solver
assertion that measures the machine as much as the solver, already recorded as flaky under
concurrent CPU load. The guard prints elapsed milliseconds per seed for budget observability and
asserts nothing whatsoever about time.

</design_decisions>
